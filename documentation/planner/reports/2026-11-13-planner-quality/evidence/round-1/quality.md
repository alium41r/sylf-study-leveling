5. **Principles of organisation** w2 d2: Order cells, tissues, organs and organ systems in organisation; Relate the structure of a tissue to its function; Describe the role of enzymes as biological catalysts; Explain enzyme action using the lock-and-key model and specificity; Interpret enzyme rate graphs for temperature, pH and substrate concentration
6. **Animal tissues, organs and organ systems** w3 d3: Describe the digestive system and the role of each digestive enzyme; Explain how the small intestine is adapted for absorption; Use qualitative reagents to test for sugars, starch, protein and lipids; Calculate and compare energy requirements from food data; Describe the heart, its blood vessels and the double circulatory system; Explain how the lungs, alveoli and blood vessels are adapted for exchange
7. **Plant tissues, organs and systems** w2 d2: Identify the tissues in a leaf and state their functions; Explain how root hair cells and xylem absorb and move water and minerals; Describe transpiration and its rate affected by temperature, light, wind and humidity; Explain translocation in the phloem and the role of guard cells and stomata
8. **Communicable diseases** w3 d2: Classify pathogens and describe methods of spreading disease; Describe symptoms, spread and control of named viral, bacterial, fungal and protist disea…; Explain how human defence systems prevent infection; Explain how vaccination produces immunity and protects populations; Compare antibiotics and painkillers and describe the problem of resistance; Describe the stages of drug discovery, testing and the role of trials
9. **Monoclonal antibodies** w1 d3: Describe how monoclonal antibodies are produced from hybridomas; Explain their uses in diagnosis, pregnancy tests and cancer treatment; Evaluate the advantages and drawbacks of monoclonal antibody treatment
10. **Plant disease** w1 d2: Describe symptoms of common plant diseases and their causes; Identify plant diseases from photographs and data; Explain physical and chemical plant defence responses; Explain how plant diseases are detected and controlled
11. **Photosynthesis** w3 d3: State the word and balanced symbol equation for photosynthesis; Describe the effect of light intensity, carbon dioxide and temperature on the rate; Interpret limiting factor graphs and suggest improvements to the practical; Explain how leaf structure is adapted for efficient photosynthesis; Explain how plants use glucose and stored starch
12. **Respiration** w2 d2: Write the word and balanced equation for aerobic respiration; Compare aerobic and anaerobic respiration in muscles and yeast; Explain the effects of exercise and how the body responds to oxygen debt; Describe how metabolism uses the products of respiration

Topics:

1. **Units and Measurements** w1 d1: Convert between SI units and use dimensional analysis to check relations; Compute errors: absolute, relative, percentage, and combination of errors; Apply significant figures and least count of instruments; Use dimensions to derive and verify physical formulae
2. **Kinematics** w3 d2: Read and interpret position-time and velocity-time graphs; Solve uniformly accelerated motion problems with kinematic equations; Analyse projectile motion: range, time of flight, maximum height; Use relative velocity in one and two dimensions; Relate angular and linear quantities in uniform circular motion
3. **Laws of Motion** w2 d2: Apply Newton's three laws and impulse-momentum theorem; Use conservation of linear momentum in collisions and explosions; Solve problems with static and kinetic friction, inclines; Analyse equilibrium of concurrent forces with free-body diagrams; Find centripetal force for vehicles on level and banked roads
4. **Work, Energy and Power** w2 d2: Compute work done by constant and variable forces; Apply the work-energy theorem to find speeds and distances; Use conservation of mechanical energy including spring potential energy; Distinguish conservative from non-conservative forces; Solve elastic and inelastic collisions in one and two dimensions
5. **Rotational Motion** (weak) w3 d3: Locate centre of mass of two-particle systems and rigid bodies; Compute torque, angular momentum and apply its conservation; Calculate moment of inertia using parallel and perpendicular axis theorems; Write rotational equations of motion and compare with linear analogues; Analyse rolling and equilibrium of rigid bodies
6. **Gravitation** w2 d2: Apply Newton's law of gravitation and find g variation with altitude and depth; Use Kepler's laws to relate orbital period and radius; Calculate gravitational potential and potential energy; Find escape velocity, orbital velocity and satellite energy
7. **Properties of Solids and Liquids** w2 d2: Use stress-strain relations, Hooke's law, Young's, bulk and rigidity moduli; Apply Pascal's law and pressure variation with depth; Solve viscosity problems using Stokes' law and terminal velocity; Analyse streamline flow, critical velocity and Bernoulli's principle; Work with surface tension, capillarity and excess pressure
8. **Thermodynamics and Kinetic Theory** w2 d2: Apply the first law to isothermal, adiabatic and cyclic processes; Compute work done and heat in thermodynamic processes; Interpret P-V and T-S diagrams for efficiency of cycles; Use the ideal gas equation and kinetic theory relations; Relate degrees of freedom to specific heats and mean free path
9. **Oscillations and Waves** w3 d3: Solve SHM problems: period, amplitude, phase and energy; Analyse pendulum and spring-mass systems; Use the wave equation to find speed, frequency and wavelength; Apply superposition, beats and standing wave conditions; Solve resonance problems in pipes and strings, Doppler effect
10. **Electrostatics** (weak) w3 d2: Use Coulomb's law and superposition for point charge systems; Compute electric field and potential of dipoles, rings and spheres; Apply Gauss's law to symmetric charge distributions; Find capacitance of combinations and energy stored; Analyse dielectrics and parallel plate capacitors
11. **Current Electricity** w2 d2: Apply Ohm's law, resistivity and temperature dependence; Solve series-parallel resistor networks and Kirchhoff's laws; Use Wheatstone bridge, metre bridge and potentiometer; Handle internal resistance, EMF and cells in series/parallel; Compute power dissipation and heating effects of current
12. **Magnetic Effects and Magnetism** w3 d2: Find magnetic field from a straight wire, loop and solenoid (Biot-Savart, Ampere); Compute force on a moving charge and on a current-carrying conductor; Analyse motion of a charge in a magnetic field, cyclotron; Apply moving coil galvanometer and conversions to ammeter/voltmeter; Solve problems on bar magnet, magnetic dipole and Earth's magnetism
13. **Electromagnetic Induction and AC** w3 d3: Apply Faraday's and Lenz's law to find induced EMF and current; Compute self and mutual inductance; energy in an inductor; Analyse AC circuits with R, L, C: impedance and resonance; Use phasor diagrams for series LCR circuits; Solve transformer and power factor problems
14. **Ray Optics and Optical Instruments** w3 d2: Use ray optics: mirror and thin lens formulae, magnification; Trace rays through prisms, optical fibres and total internal reflection; Solve refraction at spherical surfaces and lens combinations; Apply Young's double slit interference condition; Use diffraction grating and polarisation (Brewster's law)
15. **Dual Nature of Matter and Radiation** w2 d2: Apply photoelectric equation to find stopping potential and threshold; Use de Broglie relation and Davisson-Germer experiment; Solve Bohr model problems: energy levels and spectral lines; Analyse nuclear decay, half-life and binding energy; Use Einstein's mass-energy relation and nuclear fission/fusion
16. **Experimental Skills** w2 d1: Identify instruments and describe measurement methods for standard experiments; Analyse errors and least counts from experimental data; Interpret results of Vernier callipers, screw gauge and simple pendulum; Apply concepts from optics and electricity experiments to data-based questions

Topics:

1. **Electrostatics** w3 d2: State and apply Gauss's law to find field of a charge distribution; Define electric potential as work done per unit charge; Relate electric field and potential gradient; Derive and use energy stored in a capacitor; Describe charging and discharging of a capacitor; Explain electric polarization of dielectrics
2. **Current Electricity** w3 d2: Apply Kirchhoff's first and second laws to circuits; Solve series and parallel network problems; Calculate power and energy dissipated in resistors; Use the potentiometer and Wheatstone bridge principles; Explain internal resistance and EMF of a cell
3. **Electromagnetism** w3 d2: Define magnetic flux density and use F = BIL sinθ; Find field due to current in a straight wire and a solenoid; Calculate torque on a current-carrying loop; Explain the working of a galvanometer and ammeter; Describe magnetic properties of materials

9. **Atomic Spectra** w2 d2: Explain Bohr's model and energy levels; Describe spectral series of hydrogen; Calculate photon energy for transitions; Distinguish emission and absorption spectra; Explain the laser principle
10. **Nuclear Physics** w3 d3: Describe radioactive decay with half-life calculations; Explain nuclear fission and fusion processes; Calculate mass defect and binding energy; Describe basic forces of nature; Explain uses and hazards of radioactivity
