# Planning Studio — current contract

**Status (13 November 2026):** implemented beside the existing planner, behind `SYLF_PLANNING_STUDIO`. On by default in development, off in production until the owner applies the migration and sets `SYLF_PLANNING_STUDIO=on`. Source, tests, schema and configuration remain authoritative; this page says where they are and what they promise. The design input is the [planner blueprint](reports/2026-11-13-planner-blueprint/README.md); the build and its evidence are in the [implementation report](reports/2026-11-13-planning-studio/README.md).

## What it is

A planning kernel. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (`deepseek-flash`) reads a compact snapshot of the student's Sylf data and writes typed operations on a **work model** (goals, work items with calibrated estimates and study methods, practices, rules, assumptions, at most two questions). A deterministic TypeScript **solver** fits the work into real free time. **Verifiers** gate every result. Every change is a **versioned proposal** the student accepts, discards or undoes, and accepted plans are **projected** into Goals, Quests and timed blocks. Most interactions never call the model.

**Exams are planned from their syllabus** (since 13 November 2026, [report](reports/2026-11-13-syllabus-planner/README.md)). The topic list (each topic with three to eight key points, its weight, difficulty and the paper's real length) comes from Sylf's checked catalog or the shared library when the model confirms it is exactly the paper asked for (see below), from the student's own list, from one model call, or without the model from the student's own syllabus nodes for the subject; with none of these the planner asks for the topics. A deterministic **study-path compiler** turns it into the whole plan: each topic revised then practised from past papers in syllabus order, mixed reviews of every three topics at least two days later, full timed papers with corrections and the weak topics they expose, and a light formula-sheet review the day before. The model never schedules an exam.

**Presets** (13 November 2026, [report](reports/2026-11-13-planner-presets/README.md)) shape every outline-based plan: **Exam** (above), **Whole course** (each topic studied then practised in order over weeks or months, every three topics reviewed two days later and recalled again about ten days later, a timed checkpoint every six topics, and the exam tail when the course ends in one), **Topic mastery** (subtopics studied and practised in the first half, recall checks at widening gaps of one, three, seven and fourteen days, a mastery test), **Skill** (stages with a lesson, practice sessions and a milestone check, on about the student's days a week at their session length, open-ended without a date), **Reading** (parts in order with written recaps) and **Assignment** (the existing breakdown). **Auto** lets the model choose; without the model a keyword rule does (`inferPreset`). Without a date, a course runs about four days a topic and a skill as many weeks as its sessions, up to a year.

**Syllabus library** (13 November 2026, [report](reports/2026-11-13-syllabus-library/README.md)). **Every exam or course request is decided by the model** (14 November 2026, [report](reports/2026-11-14-syllabus-matching/README.md)). The request and every stored syllabus are reduced to one vocabulary of search terms (`syllabusTerms`: `board:caie`, `code:9709`, `paper:1`, `w:mathematics`), and one indexed search of both stores (the checked catalog's inverted index and the library's GIN-indexed `terms` column, `syllabusCandidates`) finds the few syllabi the request might mean. The identify call (`syllabus.identify`) names the syllabus and says which of those candidates, if any, is exactly it: same board, qualification, subject (a variant such as Further Mathematics is a different subject) and edition, and together exactly the papers asked for. Its choice must agree with what it identified (`agreesWith`: codes, boards, subject words and papers) or it is refused. A confirmed catalog paper or library entry is reused as stored, with no research; the exact library entry for the identified syllabus is used when the search did not show it. Library keys keep every paper named (`caie|9231|p1+p2`). Without the model, only a strict catalog match (board, level, subject and paper all named, never a variant) is trusted. A syllabus nobody has planned before is researched with care (three searches, up to five pages), mapped from the sources, double-checked independently against them (`syllabus.verify`, corrections bounded by `boundCorrections`) and saved for everyone. The double-check first lists the official units; any the outline still does not cover (`uncoveredUnits`) gets one focused second look, and an outline researched outside the library (an exam or course the identify step did not name) is double-checked the same way. Coursework is never a topic (`dropCoursework`), whatever the learner singles out ("especially Bayes") is always covered and marked weak (`coverFocus`), and a book outlined in fewer than ten parts is looked up for its chapters. The student's own topic list comes first and is unchanged; Sylf's hand-checked catalog is never changed by a report. After every plan, a small closeable card asks "Is this plan right?": *Looks good* records it (`PlanFeedback`) and changes nothing; *Something's off* runs `recheck` (research again, map again told the last outline was reported wrong, double-check against sources and the note, rebuild as a proposal in a new round), and a correction confirmed against sources becomes the shared entry's next version.

**Research** is optional and the model's call: the outline map returns a confidence and, when it is not high, a search query. Where a search provider is configured (`selectSearchProvider`: DeepSeek Search, then Brave, Serper or SearXNG) the server searches once, reads up to three pages with `fetchSource`, and maps again from them; the syllabus records `researched` and its sources. `SYLF_PLANNING_STUDIO_RESEARCH=off` switches it off.

## The kernel's promises

- **Determinism.** Integer minutes, 5-minute grid, integer flow costs, seeded local search: identical inputs give identical plans on the server and the device.
- **Hard verifiers** (a failing plan is never proposed): reference integrity, no overlap with commitments or other sessions, inside free time and windows, rest days and days off honoured, dependencies in order (spaced methods at least two days apart on shared topics), daily and per-subject caps, every deadline met or reported as an exact shortage, and every demanded minute placed, budgeted to a later week or reported unplaced.
- **Critics** (scored, shown under "How this was planned"): realism against sustainable capacity, exam preparation (share of topics with two spaced retrievals, a light review the day before, no new learning in the final day), front-loading with p80 estimates, interleaving, fragmentation, neglected goals.
- **Modes.** `full` re-solves with a stability term (changes in meaning: new goals, rules, what-ifs) and ends with a fill pass. `repair` keeps every committed session that is still valid exactly where it is and places only the residual (missed, skipped, invalidated or new work). An unchanged world repairs without moving, resizing or removing anything.
- **Imminent sessions** (starting within 30 minutes) and pinned, done, missed and skipped sessions never move.
- **Explanations** come from the solver's trace (`reasons`), never from the model's recollection.
- **Study paths** (`compileStudyPath`) are deterministic. Sessions have stable keys (`<goal>.rev.u3`, `<goal>.paper.1`), so re-compiling after a rating or an unticked topic updates the same sessions, keeps their quests and never touches studied work; unstarted sessions that are no longer wanted are released, and older non-syllabus work of the goal is released on the first compile. Pacing is by date windows from today to the day before the exam over the student's study days; when time is short, sessions shrink to a floor and the plan says so. The rule "no new learning in the final 24 hours" is measured from the exam date, not from a session's own window.
- **Horizon.** The detailed horizon reaches an active exam up to 28 days away (counting an open proposal's exams), so a near exam's whole path lands in the calendar instead of in weekly budgets. Beyond the horizon, weekly budgets reach 52 weeks, so a course can be planned to its end; the study guide lists those weeks as "Later".
- **Hours a day** (revised 13 November 2026, [quality report](reports/2026-11-13-planner-quality/README.md)). A plan's own minutes a day become a `goal_cap` constraint on its sessions at exactly that number (never above the account's daily cap), and it holds on every day and, beyond the horizon, in every week (at most the limit on each of the week's study days). A single sitting longer than the limit, a full timed paper, is a **solo sitting**: allowed, and it takes its day to itself in that cap group (`capGroups().soloOf`, enforced in allocation, consolidation, repair and the verifier). Without the student's number, exams, courses, topics and books take a default limit (2 h 30 m, 1 h, 1 h and 45 m; none for a test one to three days away), recorded with provenance `default`. A student's own minutes a day also lift the solver's calibrated comfort level for that plan, so six hours a day is planned as asked rather than as a new user's average. Cap groups are a goal's own limit for its sessions and a subject's limit for every other session of that subject; each item counts against at most one.
- **Using the time offered** (same date). When the student gives minutes a day and the content alone would leave most of them empty, exam and course plans grow to about 80% of what is offered on the learning days (55% under a default limit): each topic is studied in parts of one session, each part a slice of its key points ("Kinematics revision (1 of 2)"), then practised, first straight after and then after widening gaps (2, 6, 13, 24, 40 days, squeezed to fit before the exam, and fewer for late topics so the last days stay with the papers). Sessions take the nearest size that packs the day (a whole, half, third or quarter of the limit). When time is far too short for a revision and a practice per topic, each topic gets one combined session; a test one or two days away gets a few quick revision sessions covering every topic and one mixed practice, all before the test day. Reading plans fill the time offered too, read long chapters over two or three sittings and pack short consecutive chapters into one.
- **Papers.** A timed paper is practised at its real length (with ten minutes to set up for papers up to two and a half hours), never longer than the account's daily cap; an exam over five hours (USMLE Step 1) is practised as four-hour blocks. Long plans sit up to eight papers, numbered per paper ("Paper 2 timed past paper 2").
- **Assignments.** The model's breakdown is sized to the real effort (reading sources, drafted words an hour, revision) in 4–16 items; a topic it names in words rather than by a snapshot ref no longer rejects the item, and items naming a later item are retried once the whole document is in. The work is then paced over the time to the date (`paceWork`): windows in proportion to each item's size, in order, leaving the last tenth free.

## Lifecycle

1. **Sense.** `compileContext` reads availability (fixed and floating rest days, the study day, calendar events through `readPlanningCalendar` including connected calendars, other quests' load), subjects and syllabus nodes, goals (Sylf goals join unplanned; goals owned by another planner are shown and never edited), linked quests (progress, completion, deletion, a student's own rescheduling), four weeks of focus history (calibration, energy curve, sustainable capacity) and the living plan. Past committed sessions become done or missed.
2. **Understand** (only here does the model run). See workflows below.
3. **Fit, check, propose.** `propose()` solves, verifies and diffs against the committed plan. A proposal is a `PlanVersion` in state `PROPOSED`; one is open at a time.
4. **Commit.** `acceptProposal` re-checks the proposal's placements against today's world. If the plan moved on or the calendar changed, it is re-solved and returned for review instead (`rebased`). Otherwise, inside `executionTransaction` (the per-student advisory lock) with the calendar planning lock: projection, block rows, version `COMMITTED`, `PlanningSpace.version + 1`. A replayed key returns the first outcome.
5. **Undo.** A new committed version restoring the previous version's intent and placements, with today's progress and history kept.
6. **Observe and repair.** `POST {action:"open"}` runs sense and repair. *Smart* (default) applies small changes by itself, *Autopilot* every non-destructive repair, *Ask me* nothing; larger repairs become proposals. The overnight deadline check calls `repairBeforeDeadline` for Studio quests before failing anything.

## Projection into Sylf

- A new kernel goal with work becomes a `Goal` (title, `targetDate`, `targetWeeks`, colour; for a syllabus goal the description names the syllabus and its topics).
- Each open work item with a session in the detailed horizon has one `Quest`: `requiredMinutes` and `targetMinutes` from the calibrated p50 (15–240), difficulty from `calculateRecommendedDifficulty`, rewards from `calculateQuestRewards`, `scheduledFor` = the date of its next session, subject and first syllabus node, `createdByAi` for model and template work, and a `description` with the session's aim, its key points and how to study (`questDescription`). Practice occurrences in the next seven days get their own quests.
- **Execution protection:** a quest with elapsed time, a session or a non-open status is never deleted or reshaped; only its date moves. Unstarted quests of released work are deleted. `plannedMinutes` stays null, so V2 execution guards do not apply. The planner never grants rewards.
- `PlanItemQuest` links items to quests; `PlanBlock` holds sessions with local date, minutes and instants.

## Workflows

| Workflow | Route | Model calls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open (sense and repair) | `POST /api/planner/studio {action:"open"}` | 0 |
| Edit by command | stream `command` | 0 on the fast path (moves, days off, illness, caps, windows, weights, start earlier, drop goal, new deadlines); otherwise `intent.parse`, then parallel `goal.decompose` for new goals |
| Add a plan with a preset | stream `add_goal` with `preset` (`auto` or one of the six), and optional `minutesPerDay`, `daysPerWeek`, `sessionMinutes`, `start`, `level`, `weak`, `target`, `topics` | As below; research adds one search and one more map call only when the model is unsure |
| Add or plan an exam | stream `add_goal` (or a command naming an exam) | 1 short identify call that also confirms a stored match (catalog or library; nothing more is needed then), 0 when the student lists topics without asking for key points; otherwise a streamed syllabus map (`syllabus.map`), and for a named syllabus not yet stored, research and a double-check. The study path is compiled, never written by the model, and the critic does not run. Without any topic list the planner asks for one instead of inventing sessions |
| Add or plan another goal | stream `add_goal` | 1 streamed decomposition, then the semantic critic off the critical path (a revised proposal replaces the open one once, if it adds work) |
| "Is this plan right?": yes, or closed | `POST {action:"feedback", goal, verdict}` | 0. Recorded for the plan round; nothing changes |
| "Is this plan right?": something's off | stream `recheck` with an optional note | Search plus two calls (map, double-check); proposed for review in a new round |
| Rate, untick or add topics; confirm the list | `POST {action:"syllabus"}`, or a command such as "I'm weak at integration", "trig isn't in my exam", "skip series" (`parseTopicCommand`, also on the device for the chip) | 0. Re-compiled and proposed for review, on top of the open proposal when the goal is still in it |
| Answer a question | stream `answer` | 1 (0 for a topic list). A question asked in the open proposal is answered on top of it |
| Drag, move, pin, skip, restore | `POST {action:"direct"}` | 0, committed at once with undo. A move always lands where the student drops it (`studentLanding`: only the clock bounds it) |
| What-if | device preview; `POST {action:"whatif"}` to keep | 0 |
| Accept, discard, undo, settings | `POST` actions | 0 |
| Why is this here? | `POST {action:"explain"}`; stream `explain` for one sentence | 0, or 1 short |
| Daily brief, open question | stream `brief`, `question` | 1 short, or a deterministic fallback |

Without a DeepSeek key, or with the `studio` quota spent, model workflows fall back to the strategy library's templates and say so.

## Model gateway

`gateway.ts` owns every call: `deepseek-flash` only; non-thinking; strict function calling on `/beta/chat/completions` with a named tool (falls back once to a named tool without strict mode); streamed tool arguments parsed element by element (`StreamingArrayParser`); per-profile deadlines; two jittered retries; a hedged duplicate after 2.5 s without a first token on interactive profiles; a circuit breaker; `user_id` = HMAC of the Sylf user id; dispatch telemetry through `observeAiDispatch` inside `withAiRequest` (feature `planner`, workload `studio.*`). Quota: `AI_QUOTAS.studio` (60 workflows a day), released when a workflow falls back. Prompts are tiered for DeepSeek's prefix cache; student text travels inside `<data>` tags.

## Native

Home → Plan → **Planner** is Plan's first view; Calendar, Quests and goals, and Classic plans follow. An empty Studio opens on the composer, as the web planner does: a preset picker (the chosen preset shown with its description, opening a list of every preset as large rows), one title field, the date with relative chips, and prompts that open one small field each (hours a day, topics, board or level, weak spots, target, start date, notes; days a week and session length for a skill). Settings sit beside the command bar; the seven-day health box is gone; while an exam is planned, the activity card shows a stage rail (your week, syllabus, study path, calendar, checks) and the topics as they are mapped. Beside the calendar, an exam's **study guide** has three views: Plan (phases with real dates), Topics (each topic's key points, a Weak/Okay/Strong rating, "In this exam", add a topic, "Topics look right") and Day by day. The inspector shows a session's aim, key points and method before why it is where it is. Sylvie's "Plan it in the Planner" hands requests to it. Layout: health strip, command bar with suggestion chips, live interpretation chip (the kernel's fast path on the device) and activity line; preview bar for drags and what-ifs; diff review; signals with pre-solved options; Day, Week, Month and Term timeline beside the goal map or inspector; What if…; history with undo; settings (autonomy, study day, daily cap, break, day-only lens). On a phone it opens on Today with the next session (Start, Later). What-ifs re-solve on the device with the same kernel; Start opens Focus with the session's quest.

**Drag and drop** (14 November 2026, [report](reports/2026-11-14-drag-and-month/README.md)). Any session not yet done, missed and skipped ones included, can be dragged by its whole body: a mouse picks it up after a few pixels, a finger after a short press, so the grid still scrolls under a swipe. It follows the pointer on the UI thread (Gesture Handler and Reanimated, no re-render while it moves), the slot it will land in is outlined with its day and time, and the grid scrolls by itself near its edges. Week and Day grids cover the whole day and open at the start of the study day. On release the session shows in its new place at once and glides there; the move commits in the order the student made it, and the rest of the plan reflows around it. **The student's placement wins**: daily and subject caps, rest days and days off, study hours, commitments, other sessions, topic order and the planning horizon never refuse a drop. Only the clock bounds it: a time already past lands at the next five minutes from now, and nothing runs past midnight (`studentLanding`, in the kernel, used by the device and the server). Repair treats a placed session as fixed: its minutes count against its plan's daily room, a placed full paper keeps its day, and the plan's own sessions make way. **Month** shows every session as a coloured chip by day, deadlines, rest days and the weekly budgets beyond the detailed calendar; weeks already over fold to a row of marks; a chip dragged to another day keeps its time; a day's number opens that day.
